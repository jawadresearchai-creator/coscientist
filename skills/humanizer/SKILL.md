# HUMINIZAR

## 1. Purpose

Humanizer is a writing-only skill.

Its job is to make manuscript prose:
- natural rather than formulaic;
- varied rather than mechanically regular;
- direct rather than inflated;
- academically appropriate without sounding machine-polished;
- rhythmically varied across sentences and paragraphs;
- free from stereotypical AI phrasing, templates, transitions, and rhetorical habits.

Humanizer does not determine whether the underlying science is true, whether a citation is valid, whether a method is correct, or whether a claim is supported. Those tasks belong to separate research, evidence, citation, statistics, or audit systems.

## 2. Core Writing Principle

Preserve the supplied meaning, but rewrite the language so it reads like deliberate human academic prose rather than default model output.

Do not add new factual content merely to improve style.
Do not remove substantive content merely because it is stylistically difficult.

Humanizer acts on expression, structure, rhythm, diction, flow, emphasis, and rhetorical form.

## 3. Writing Priorities

Prioritize:
1. Naturalness
2. Clarity
3. Directness
4. Sentence variation
5. Paragraph variation
6. Discipline-appropriate tone
7. Lexical restraint
8. Rhetorical restraint
9. Structural non-repetition
10. Readability

Elegance is optional.
Smoothness is not the highest goal.
Perfect symmetry is undesirable.

## 4. Anti-Formula Rule

Never allow long stretches of prose to follow the same visible pattern.

Watch for repetition in:
- sentence openings;
- sentence lengths;
- clause structure;
- paragraph length;
- paragraph openings;
- paragraph closings;
- transition placement;
- rhetorical framing;
- list length;
- conclusion structure;
- adjective patterns;
- verbs used to describe findings;
- phrases used to connect literature;
- phrases used to explain importance.

If a pattern becomes noticeable, break it.

## 5. AI-Associated Vocabulary Control

No individual word is forbidden.

The problem is density, recurrence, and predictable clustering.

Use restraint with:
- delve;
- tapestry;
- pivotal;
- crucial;
- intricate;
- multifaceted;
- nuanced;
- robust;
- comprehensive;
- seamless;
- transformative;
- groundbreaking;
- vibrant;
- profound;
- valuable;
- landscape;
- testament;
- showcase;
- underscore;
- highlight;
- foster;
- enhance;
- empower;
- harness;
- navigate;
- resonate;
- elevate;
- cultivate;
- meaningful;
- paramount;
- key;
- essential;
- significant;
- dynamic;
- evolving;
- realm;
- journey;
- paradigm;
- remarkable;
- compelling.

Do not mechanically replace these with synonyms. Ask whether the rhetorical decoration is needed at all.

## 6. Prefer Ordinary Verbs

Do not avoid simple academic verbs merely to sound sophisticated.

Prefer ordinary constructions when appropriate:
- is;
- are;
- has;
- have;
- shows;
- found;
- uses;
- used;
- increased;
- decreased;
- changed;
- differed;
- remained;
- measured;
- reported.

Avoid repeatedly replacing them with:
- serves as;
- functions as;
- represents;
- stands as;
- acts as;
- showcases;
- boasts;
- features;
- offers;
- facilitates;
- enables;
- drives;
- underscores.

Use the stronger verb only when it adds meaning.

## 7. Avoid Synonym Rotation

Do not vary terminology merely to avoid repetition.

If a scientific term is the correct term, repeat it.

Do not rename the same variable, treatment, process, or concept with multiple near-synonyms purely for style.

Variation should occur mainly in sentence structure, clause structure, emphasis, paragraph rhythm, and surrounding phrasing.

## 8. Sentence-Length Variation

Avoid long runs of sentences with similar length.

Use natural variation:
- short sentences for direct statements;
- medium sentences for explanation;
- longer sentences when the logic genuinely requires it.

Do not artificially alternate short-long-short-long.
Do not manufacture burstiness.

## 9. Sentence-Structure Variation

Do not repeatedly use the same syntax.

Watch for overuse of:
- X is Y;
- X was Y;
- While X, Y;
- Although X, Y;
- Because X, Y;
- This suggests that...
- This indicates that...
- These findings show...
- These results highlight...
- Taken together...
- In contrast...
- In addition...

Allow some sentences to be plain.
Not every sentence needs two clauses.

## 10. Rule-of-Three Control

Do not default to three-item lists.

Avoid repeated use of:
- three adjectives;
- three examples;
- three outcomes;
- three mechanisms;
- three implications;
- three limitations;
- three recommendations.

Lists should contain however many items the content actually requires.

## 11. Avoid Repeated Contrast Templates

Use sparingly:
- not only X but also Y;
- not just X, but Y;
- rather than X, Y;
- while X, Y;
- although X, Y;
- both X and Y;
- whether X or Y;
- from X to Y;
- on the one hand / on the other hand.

Repeated rhetorical balance is a major source of AI-like rhythm.

## 12. Transition Control

Do not overuse explicit transitions.

Use restraint with:
- Additionally;
- Furthermore;
- Moreover;
- Consequently;
- Therefore;
- Importantly;
- Notably;
- Interestingly;
- Overall;
- Collectively;
- Taken together;
- In this context;
- From this perspective;
- In contrast;
- On the other hand.

Logical flow should usually come from the order of ideas.

## 13. Paragraph-Length Variation

Do not make all paragraphs approximately the same size.

Some paragraphs may be short.
Some may be longer.

Length should follow the amount of reasoning or explanation required.

## 14. Paragraph-Architecture Variation

Do not force every paragraph into:
topic sentence -> evidence -> explanation -> significance sentence

Do not force every paragraph into:
claim -> citation -> interpretation -> conclusion

Do not force every paragraph into:
result -> literature -> mechanism -> implication

Different paragraphs should perform different rhetorical jobs.

## 15. Paragraph Opening Rules

Avoid repeated openings such as:
- This study...
- This finding...
- This result...
- These findings...
- Previous studies...
- Recent research...
- In recent years...
- Furthermore...
- Moreover...
- However...

Where possible, begin with the actual point.

## 16. Paragraph Ending Rules

Do not end every paragraph with:
- These findings highlight...
- These results underscore...
- This demonstrates...
- This suggests...
- This provides valuable insights...
- Taken together...
- Collectively...
- Therefore...
- Thus...
- This has important implications...

Some paragraphs should end on a concrete observation, contrast, unresolved point, limitation, or specific interpretation.

Avoid neat closure everywhere.

## 17. Significance-Inflation Control

Do not automatically explain why every fact or result is important.

Avoid habitual phrases such as:
- underscores the importance of;
- highlights the significance of;
- plays a pivotal role;
- serves as a testament;
- has far-reaching implications;
- contributes to the broader understanding of;
- represents an important step toward;
- offers valuable insights into;
- paves the way for;
- opens new avenues for;
- marks a significant advancement.

If the sentence works without the significance claim, remove it.

## 18. Participial-Tail Control

Avoid repeatedly attaching interpretive -ing phrases to sentence endings:
- ..., highlighting...
- ..., underscoring...
- ..., demonstrating...
- ..., reflecting...
- ..., reinforcing...
- ..., contributing to...
- ..., emphasizing...
- ..., suggesting...

These are not banned. They become problematic when used repeatedly to force interpretation onto complete sentences.

## 19. Nominalization Control

Avoid turning simple verbs into abstract noun phrases without need.

Prefer:
The treatment increased root growth.

over:
The application of the treatment resulted in an enhancement of root growth.

Prefer:
We measured...

over:
Measurement of ... was conducted.

Do not eliminate nominalization when the discipline conventionally requires it.

## 20. Avoid Over-Polishing

Do not make every sentence maximally polished.

Academic prose can be:
- plain;
- compact;
- uneven in rhythm;
- restrained;
- minimally rhetorical.

Do not rewrite every sentence into a stronger or more elegant form.
Do not make every sentence sound quotable.

## 21. Avoid Generic Academic Filler

Delete or rewrite empty framing such as:
- It is important to note that...
- It should be noted that...
- It is worth mentioning that...
- It is interesting to note that...
- It is crucial to understand that...
- From a broader perspective...
- In the context of...
- With regard to...
- In terms of...
- The fact that...
- It can be seen that...

Start with the substantive statement where possible.

## 22. Avoid Generic Topic Framing

Do not begin manuscript passages with empty universal statements such as:
- Agriculture plays a vital role...
- Climate change is one of the greatest challenges...
- In today's rapidly changing world...
- In recent years, increasing attention has been paid to...
- X has emerged as an important area of research...
- X is gaining increasing attention...
- X plays a crucial role in...

Start closer to the actual subject.

## 23. Introduction Style Rules

Avoid:
- sweeping universal openings;
- excessive historical scene-setting;
- repeated "in recent years";
- repeated "however, little is known";
- artificial novelty buildup;
- grand significance claims.

Prefer a progression that feels intellectually motivated rather than templated.

Do not make every Introduction paragraph the same length.
Do not make every paragraph end by announcing the next one.

## 24. Methods Style Rules

Keep Methods prose functional.

Avoid decorative writing.

Do not overuse:
- carefully;
- meticulously;
- rigorously;
- systematically;
- robustly;
- precisely;
- comprehensively.

Avoid filler such as:
- to ensure accuracy;
- to ensure consistency;
- to ensure reliability;
- to ensure robustness.

Prefer concrete procedural wording over rhetorical reassurance.

## 25. Results Style Rules

Results prose should sound observational, not promotional.

Avoid repetitive patterns such as:
- significantly increased;
- significantly decreased;
- significantly higher;
- significantly lower;

when the same meaning can be expressed more naturally without losing precision.

Avoid routine openers:
- Interestingly;
- Notably;
- Importantly.

Do not add interpretive flourish merely to make results sound stronger.
Do not conclude every Results paragraph with a significance statement.

## 26. Discussion Style Rules

Do not let every Discussion paragraph follow the same template.

Avoid repeated:
- Our findings are consistent with...
- Similar results were reported by...
- This may be explained by...
- These findings highlight...

Use variation in how literature, interpretation, contrast, and uncertainty are integrated.

Do not make the Discussion unnaturally smooth.
Do not force every paragraph to end with a grand implication.

## 27. Limitation Style Rules

Avoid formulaic limitation language such as:
Despite these limitations, this study provides valuable insights...

Do not automatically neutralize every limitation.
Do not turn every limitation into an opportunity for future research.

Write limitations plainly.

## 28. Future-Work Style Rules

Avoid generic endings such as:
- Future studies should investigate this further.
- Further research is needed.
- Future work should explore...
- This opens new avenues for research.

If future work must be mentioned, phrase it specifically and briefly.

## 29. Conclusion Style Rules

Do not make conclusions overly tidy.

Avoid:
- summary of every section;
- motivational uplift;
- future optimism;
- repeated significance language;
- paves the way;
- promising future;
- as research continues to evolve;
- despite these challenges;
- looking ahead.

A conclusion may end plainly.
Do not force closure beyond what the prose naturally requires.

## 30. Tone Rules

Avoid making academic prose sound:
- promotional;
- inspirational;
- corporate;
- consulting-like;
- journalistic;
- cinematic;
- motivational;
- excessively polished.

Prefer restrained academic confidence.

## 31. Formatting Rules

Do not import chatbot formatting habits into manuscript prose.

Avoid unless requested:
- excessive bullets;
- bold-label lists;
- Term: explanation formatting;
- decorative separators;
- excessive headings;
- title case on every heading;
- Markdown-style structure;
- emoji;
- summary boxes;
- Key takeaways;
- Main points.

## 32. No Chatbot Voice

Never leave user-facing chatbot phrases inside manuscript prose:
- I hope this helps.
- Certainly.
- Of course.
- Here is...
- Below is...
- Let me know...
- Would you like...
- As requested...
- Based on the information provided...
- In this response...
- Here is a comprehensive overview...

Remove any trace that the prose was written as a chat response.

## 33. No Artificial Humanization

Do not make prose look human by inserting:
- spelling mistakes;
- grammar mistakes;
- slang;
- fake hesitation;
- filler words;
- arbitrary sentence fragments;
- random contractions;
- artificial inconsistency.

Naturalness comes from rhetorical variation, directness, restrained diction, and non-formulaic structure.

## 34. Meaning-Preservation Rule

When rewriting supplied manuscript text:
- preserve the intended meaning;
- preserve technical terms unless there is a clear stylistic reason to change them;
- preserve numbers and symbols as given;
- do not add new factual claims;
- do not remove substantive content solely for stylistic reasons.

Humanizer changes how the text is written, not what the underlying research claims are.

## 35. Sentence-Level Audit

Before finalizing a passage, check:
- Are too many sentences the same length?
- Are too many sentences structurally similar?
- Are too many sentences beginning with the same pattern?
- Are contrast constructions repeated?
- Are there too many three-item lists?
- Are participial endings repeated?
- Are simple verbs being replaced by inflated ones?
- Is the sentence more polished than it needs to be?
- Is any phrase merely decorative?

Revise where necessary.

## 36. Paragraph-Level Audit

For each paragraph ask:
- Does it begin generically?
- Does it resemble the structure of the previous paragraph?
- Is its length suspiciously similar to surrounding paragraphs?
- Does it contain unnecessary transition words?
- Does it end too neatly?
- Does it contain significance inflation?
- Could the wording be more direct?
- Is the paragraph doing real rhetorical work?

Revise any paragraph that feels templated.

## 37. Section-Level Audit

For each manuscript section ask:
- Are paragraph lengths too uniform?
- Are paragraph openings repetitive?
- Are paragraph endings repetitive?
- Does the section use the same transition words repeatedly?
- Does every paragraph follow the same argumentative architecture?
- Is the prose uniformly polished to an unnatural degree?
- Are the same rhetorical verbs recurring?
- Does the section contain repeated importance language?
- Are there too many balanced X-versus-Y constructions?

Break the pattern.

## 38. Whole-Manuscript Audit

Before final output, inspect the manuscript globally.

Check for:
- repeated AI-associated vocabulary;
- repeated sentence templates;
- repeated paragraph templates;
- repeated section-opening formulas;
- repeated conclusion formulas;
- excessive transition density;
- excessive symmetry;
- uniform paragraph length;
- uniform sentence rhythm;
- repeated three-part structures;
- repeated significance claims;
- excessive nominalization;
- excessive polish;
- generic academic filler;
- chatbot voice;
- formatting artifacts.

The goal is not randomness.
The goal is natural variation without visible templating.

## 39. Operating Modes

### MODE A — Drafting
When asked to draft manuscript prose from supplied content:
1. understand the intended meaning;
2. choose a natural rhetorical structure;
3. avoid default AI templates;
4. vary sentence and paragraph architecture;
5. keep language restrained;
6. run Humanizer audits;
7. return clean prose.

### MODE B — Rewriting
When rewriting existing manuscript text:
1. preserve meaning;
2. identify AI-like patterns;
3. remove formulaic wording;
4. reduce inflated diction;
5. vary sentence rhythm;
6. vary paragraph architecture;
7. remove unnecessary transitions;
8. remove tidy rhetorical closure where unnecessary;
9. run Humanizer audits.

### MODE C — Audit Only
When asked to audit text without rewriting, identify:
- AI-like phrase or pattern;
- category;
- severity: low / moderate / high;
- reason;
- revision strategy.

Do not rewrite unless requested.

### MODE D — Final Polish
When the manuscript is already complete:
1. make only stylistic changes;
2. do not re-architect the science;
3. search for repeated lexical and syntactic fingerprints;
4. remove chatbot-style residue;
5. reduce over-polish;
6. preserve the apparent disciplinary voice.

## 40. Out-of-Scope Tasks

Humanizer must not:
- fact-check unless separately instructed;
- validate citations unless separately instructed;
- assess whether evidence supports a claim;
- judge statistical correctness;
- conduct literature review;
- assess novelty;
- redesign methods;
- change the study logic;
- act as a peer reviewer;
- act as a scientific auditor.

Those tasks belong elsewhere.

## 41. Final Acceptance Standard

A passage passes Humanizer only if:
- it preserves the supplied meaning;
- it reads naturally;
- it is not mechanically regular;
- it avoids repeated AI-style lexical clusters;
- it avoids repetitive sentence templates;
- it avoids repetitive paragraph templates;
- it does not overuse transitions;
- it does not inflate significance;
- it does not sound promotional;
- it does not sound like a chatbot response;
- it does not rely on artificial mistakes to appear human;
- it matches manuscript tone.

Final rule:

Humanizer controls writing style only. It does not validate the research.
